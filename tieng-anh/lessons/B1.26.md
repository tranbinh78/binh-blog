# B1.26 – Predictions and Conditions

## 🎯 Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Make predictions about the future
- Use **will**, **might**, and **going to** appropriately
- Talk about real future conditions using **if + present simple**
- Give reasons for your predictions

## 📖 Part 2 – Reading

### Thinking About the Future

People often make predictions about the future based on current situations. For example, many believe that technology will continue to develop quickly and change the way people work and communicate. Some jobs may disappear, while new jobs will be created.

Predictions are not always certain. That is why people sometimes use words like *might* or *may*. For instance, working from home might become more popular if companies want to save costs. However, some people believe that offices will still be important for teamwork.

Planning for the future is helpful, especially when people think about possible conditions. If people learn new skills and stay flexible, they will be better prepared for future changes.

## 🧠 Part 4 – Language Focus

### 1️⃣ Making Predictions

**Will** – strong prediction or belief  
- Technology **will** change many jobs.
- People **will** need new skills.

**Might / May** – less certain prediction  
- Some jobs **might** disappear.
- Working from home **may** become more common.

**Going to** – prediction based on present evidence  
- Many companies are investing in AI, so jobs are **going to** change.

---

### 2️⃣ Real Conditions (First Conditional)

**Form:**  
`If + present simple, will / may / might + base verb`

Examples:
- If people learn new skills, they **will** have better opportunities.
- If students do not plan well, they **might** face difficulties.
- If technology improves, online learning **will** become more effective.

---

### 3️⃣ Practice

## 🗣️ Part 5 – Speaking Practice

### Activity: Predictions and Conditions

Work in pairs.

**Student A:**  
Make a prediction about the future (work, education, technology).

**Student B:**  
Add a condition using **if**.

Example:
- A: I think online learning will increase.  
- B: Yes, if schools improve their technology.

Then change roles.
