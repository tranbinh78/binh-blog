# B1.14 – Giving Advice for Health

## 🎯 Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Give health advice in common situations
- Use structures such as **should / shouldn’t**, **have to**, and **if** clauses
- Speak politely and clearly when giving suggestions

## 📖 Part 2 – Reading

### Giving Advice for a Healthier Life

Many people want to live a healthier life, but they are not sure what to do. Giving good advice can help others improve their habits and feel better.

For example, if someone feels tired all the time, they should try to get enough sleep and take short breaks during the day. People who are stressed at work should relax more and avoid working too many hours without rest.

Eating well is also important. If people eat too much fast food, they should choose healthier meals such as vegetables and fruit. Small changes like these can help people feel healthier and more positive.

## 🧠 Part 4 – Language Focus

### 1️⃣ Giving Advice with **Should / Shouldn’t**

We use **should / shouldn’t** to give advice.

Examples:
- You **should** get enough sleep.
- You **shouldn’t** eat too much fast food.
- People **should** exercise regularly.

---

### 2️⃣ Giving Advice with **If**

We use **if** to talk about situations and results.

Examples:
- **If** you feel tired, you should take a break.
- **If** people eat healthy food, they feel better.

---

### 3️⃣ Other Useful Expressions

- It’s a good idea to …
- You might want to …
- Why don’t you …?

Examples:
- It’s a good idea to drink more water.
- You might want to walk every day.
- Why don’t you see a doctor?

## 🗣️ Part 5 – Speaking Practice

### Activity: Giving Health Advice

Work in pairs.

**Student A:**  
Describe a health problem (tiredness, stress, poor sleep, unhealthy eating).

**Student B:**  
Give **at least two pieces of advice** using:
- should / shouldn’t
- if
- other advice expressions

Then change roles.
